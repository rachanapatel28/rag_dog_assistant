Dogs and children can form wonderful bonds, but safe interactions require supervision and teaching on both sides. Children should learn to be gentle, to avoid disturbing a dog that is eating, sleeping, or hiding, and never to hug, climb on, or corner a dog, since these are common triggers for bites. Adults should watch for canine stress signals such as lip-licking, yawning, turning away, or a tucked tail, and intervene calmly before the dog feels forced to escalate. Dogs should always have a safe space children cannot follow them into. Interactions between young children and dogs should never be left entirely unsupervised, however trusted the dog.
