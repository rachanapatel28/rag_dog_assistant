Separation anxiety is genuine distress a dog feels when left alone, and it is different from boredom or mischief. Signs include destructive behaviour focused on exits, excessive vocalising, house-soiling despite training, and pacing or drooling that begin soon after the owner leaves. It is more common in dogs strongly bonded to their people, including some Retrievers and rescue dogs. Management focuses on gradually building tolerance to being alone, providing enrichment, avoiding dramatic departures and arrivals, and in serious cases working with a trainer or veterinary behaviourist. Punishment worsens the anxiety; the aim is to help the dog feel safe alone.
