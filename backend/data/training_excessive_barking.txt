Barking is normal communication, but excessive barking usually has an identifiable cause that must be addressed rather than simply suppressed. Common triggers include boredom, alarm at passers-by, attention-seeking, fear, and separation distress, and the right response depends on the cause. Meeting a dog's exercise and enrichment needs reduces boredom barking, while managing the environment, such as blocking window views, reduces alarm barking. Avoid rewarding attention-seeking barking by giving in to it, and never rely on punishment devices, which can increase fear and aggression. For persistent or anxiety-driven barking, a qualified trainer or veterinary behaviourist can help identify and treat the underlying reason.
