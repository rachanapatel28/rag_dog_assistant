Dogs are far more vulnerable to heat than people because they cool themselves mainly by panting, and heatstroke can be fatal within minutes. Never leave a dog in a parked car, even briefly, as temperatures rise dangerously fast. Exercise should be limited to the cooler parts of the day, and hot pavement can burn paw pads, so surfaces should be tested with a hand. Brachycephalic breeds, thick-coated northern breeds, elderly, overweight, and very young dogs are at especially high risk. Signs of heatstroke include heavy panting, drooling, weakness, and collapse; affected dogs need immediate cooling and emergency veterinary care.
