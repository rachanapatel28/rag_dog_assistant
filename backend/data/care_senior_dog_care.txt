Senior dogs, generally those in the last third of their expected lifespan, have changing needs that attentive care can meet well. They often slow down, sleep more, and may develop arthritis, dental disease, reduced hearing or vision, and organ changes, so more frequent veterinary checks help catch issues early. Diet may need adjusting to maintain a healthy weight and support joints, and exercise should continue but be gentler. Comfortable bedding, easier access to favourite spots, and patience with age-related changes improve quality of life. Many behavioural changes in older dogs have medical causes worth investigating rather than dismissing as "just age."
