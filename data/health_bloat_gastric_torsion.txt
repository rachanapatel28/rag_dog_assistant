Bloat, medically called gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV), is a sudden life-threatening emergency most common in large, deep-chested breeds such as Great Danes, German Shepherds, and Weimaraners. The stomach fills with gas and can twist, cutting off blood supply, and a dog can die within hours without emergency surgery. Warning signs include a swollen or hard abdomen, unproductive retching or attempts to vomit, restlessness, drooling, and signs of pain or distress. This requires immediate veterinary attention; it is never something to wait out. Risk may be reduced by feeding smaller meals and avoiding vigorous exercise right after eating, and at-risk breeds' owners should learn the signs.
