Socialisation means positively exposing a dog, especially a puppy, to a wide variety of people, animals, environments, sounds, and experiences so it grows into a confident, well-adjusted adult. The prime window is early puppyhood, roughly up to sixteen weeks, when experiences shape lifelong reactions, though socialisation continues throughout life. Exposures should be gentle and positive, never overwhelming, so the dog builds good associations rather than fear. Poor socialisation is a major cause of fear-based aggression and anxiety in adult dogs. Puppy classes, calm introductions, and gradual new experiences all help build a stable temperament.
