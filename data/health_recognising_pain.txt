Dogs instinctively hide pain, so owners must watch for subtle signs rather than expecting obvious distress. Indicators can include reduced activity, reluctance to jump or climb stairs, changes in appetite, altered posture, restlessness or difficulty settling, excessive licking of a body part, changes in temperament such as irritability, and quieter or more withdrawn behaviour. Panting unrelated to heat or exercise can also signal discomfort. Because these signs are easy to dismiss as ageing or mood, any persistent change is worth a veterinary check. Importantly, never give human painkillers to manage suspected pain, as many are toxic; a vet can provide safe pain relief. This is general information, not a diagnosis.
