Cold tolerance varies widely by breed, coat, size, and age. Thick double-coated breeds like Huskies and Samoyeds handle cold well, while thin-coated, small, elderly, or very young dogs feel it acutely and may need coats and limited time outdoors. Signs a dog is too cold include shivering, reluctance to move, and lifting paws. Winter hazards include frostbite on extremities, ice-melt salts that irritate and are toxic if licked from paws, and antifreeze, which is sweet-tasting and deadly even in small amounts. Wiping paws after walks and providing warm, draught-free shelter keep dogs safe in winter.
