Resource guarding is when a dog protects food, toys, or spaces with stiffening, growling, or snapping, and it is a natural instinct rather than "bad" behaviour. A growl is valuable communication and should never be punished, as suppressing warnings can lead to bites without warning. Mild guarding can be improved by teaching the dog that people approaching its resources predicts good things, for example by adding a better treat rather than taking things away. Prevention includes not disturbing a dog while eating and teaching children to respect this. Serious or escalating guarding, especially around people, warrants guidance from a qualified force-free trainer or veterinary behaviourist.
