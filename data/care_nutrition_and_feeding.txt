Good nutrition is built on a complete, balanced diet appropriate to the dog's life stage, size, and activity level, fed in measured portions to prevent obesity. Obesity is one of the most common and preventable health problems in dogs and shortens lifespan while worsening joint, heart, and metabolic conditions. Owners should follow feeding guidelines as a starting point and adjust based on body condition, keeping the dog lean enough to feel the ribs easily. Some foods safe for humans are toxic to dogs, including chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, and the sweetener xylitol. Fresh water should always be available, and diet changes should be made gradually.
